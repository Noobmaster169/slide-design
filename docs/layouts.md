# Layouts

Pick per slide; the claim decides. These cover most decks.

| Layout | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | the thesis, a turn in the argument | one claim, 70 to 90px, nothing else competing |
| Statement + illustration | a claim a picture can argue | picture argues, never decorates |
| Cards (2 to 4) | parallel points of equal weight | five cards means two slides |
| Step flow | a process or pipeline | number the steps and show what each produces |
| Layered stack | "what sits above what" | only when layering is the point |
| Hub and spokes | one thing and its relationships | under eight spokes |
| Chart + hero number | a result | one chart, one number, two supporting stats |
| Screenshot + notes | proof that something exists | real screenshots, never mockups posing as real |
| Table | dense comparison | if it needs more than six rows it is a handout, not a slide |
| Quote | someone else's words | attribute exactly, never invent |
| Section divider | a change of subject | keep them rare; they are not content |

## Vertical rhythm

On a 1080px slide: title block ends by ~200, content band 230 to 900, footer ~1010. Content that
stops at 700 leaves a dead band, which `slides check` reports as a note. Either grow it, centre it,
or cut the slide.

## Density

One idea per slide is a guideline, not a law. A technical audience reading a detailed diagram is
fine; the same density behind a speaker who is also talking is not. Decide who is reading and when.
